AIR

Adaptive Inference Runtime

AIR is a local-first inference runtime built around explicit architecture, observable runtime state, bounded scheduling, and real qualification. It is an open Superior MI Labs research effort focused on transparent, high-trust engineering rather than black-box hype.

What stands out

Why AIR is interesting

AIR is not just another wrapper around a model endpoint. The point is a more legible and more controlled runtime surface: visible state, explicit structure, bounded execution, and public engineering that others can inspect.

Observable runtime state

Expose what the runtime is doing instead of hiding behavior behind opaque abstractions.

Bounded scheduling

Favor clear execution boundaries and qualification over vague “agentic” behavior.

Decision visibility

Make inference, decision, and scoring surfaces explicit enough to inspect and compare.

Research-driven engineering

Use public releases to communicate architecture, implementation, and evidence clearly.

Design intent

AIR is meant to feel inspectable. The runtime surface should tell you what it is doing, what assumptions it is making, and where the logic lives. That is the trust model.

Why local-first matters

Local-first inference gives individuals, researchers, and organizations more control over cost, privacy, latency, and infrastructure behavior.

Why explicit architecture matters

Explicit contracts and bounded execution make testing, debugging, and qualification easier than opaque runtime stacks.
